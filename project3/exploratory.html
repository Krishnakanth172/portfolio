Wealth & Aging: How Does Prosperity Relate to an Older Population?

Exploratory analysis using World Bank health and economy indicators from 1990–2020.

Research question: How does GDP per capita relate to the share of a country's population aged 65+, and how has this relationship changed from 1990 to 2020?

1. How has the share of adults aged 65+ changed over time?

Unweighted average percentage of the population aged 65 and above across countries with available data.

2. Are wealthier countries more likely to have older populations?

GDP per capita compared with the percentage of the population aged 65 and above in 2020.

3. Does country population change how we interpret wealth and aging?

GDP per capita versus population age 65+ in 2020, with bubble size representing total population.

4. How much did countries age between 1990 and 2020?

Each point compares a country's share of population aged 65+ in 1990 with its value in 2020. Points above the diagonal indicate an increase.

5. Which countries had the oldest populations in 2020?

The fifteen countries with the largest percentage of residents aged 65 and above.

6. Did countries that became wealthier also become older?

Change in GDP per capita from 1990 to 2020 compared with the change in the share of the population aged 65 and above.

Data source: World Bank Data by Indicators, course-provided pre-cleaned health and economy-and-growth datasets.
