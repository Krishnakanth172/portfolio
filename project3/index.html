Design Rationale

This visualization explores how economic prosperity relates to population aging across countries from 1990 to 2020. GDP per capita is encoded on the horizontal axis using a logarithmic scale because values vary substantially across countries, while the percentage of the population aged 65 and above is encoded on the vertical axis. Each country is represented by a circle, with circle size encoding total population.

I chose a bubble scatterplot because position provides a direct way to compare the relationship between two quantitative variables while population size adds useful context without requiring a separate chart. I considered separate country-level line charts and small multiples, but these made cross-country comparison more difficult and increased visual complexity.

The year slider acts as a dynamic query that lets the reader examine how the relationship changes over time. Hover tooltips provide details on demand, while country selection highlights one country and displays its historical trajectory across the full period. The remaining countries are deemphasized so the selected trajectory can be interpreted without losing the global context.

A restrained blue palette is used for the country bubbles so the overall distribution remains visually calm, while orange is reserved for the selected country and its historical trajectory. This creates a clear contrast between context and focus without assigning categorical meaning to countries through many competing colors.

Data Preparation

The visualization uses the course-provided World Bank health and economy-and-growth datasets. I merged the datasets by country, country code, and year; restricted the analysis to 1990–2020; removed aggregate World Bank regions; and excluded observations missing GDP per capita, population, or population-age-65+ values.

The final visualization uses GDP per capita in constant 2017 international dollars, total population, and the percentage of each country's population aged 65 and above.

Development Process

I completed this project individually with instructor approval. I spent approximately 12–15 hours on the project, including dataset inspection and preprocessing, exploratory analysis, D3 implementation, interaction design, debugging, responsive styling, and deployment.

The most time-consuming parts were combining and validating the World Bank indicators, designing interactions that remained understandable across many countries, and implementing the selected-country trajectory while keeping the visualization responsive.

Six exploratory visualizations were created first to evaluate temporal aging trends, the wealth-aging relationship, population effects, country-level change, the oldest populations, and changes in wealth alongside changes in aging. These explorations led to the final interactive question and bubble-chart design.
